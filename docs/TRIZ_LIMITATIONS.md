# TRIZ Layer — Limitations and Non-Claims

1. **Does not prove truth**  
   Outputs are structural signals on a graph derived from text and dictionaries. They are not verification of factual claims.

2. **Does not replace expert judgment**  
   Analysts must interpret scores, clusters, and routes in domain context.

3. **Depends on upstream quality**  
   Dictionary coverage, concept resolution, and relation extraction quality directly bound usefulness.

4. **Bounded by corpus**  
   Sparse or biased corpora produce sparse or biased graphs; metrics reflect the input, not the world.

5. **Signal analytics, not epistemic certainty**  
   Tension, innovation pressure, and narrative metrics describe **patterns in the supplied representation**, not objective social or political reality.

6. **Benchmarks are stability tools**  
   Profile-fit scores support repeatable demos and regression checks; they are not scientific validation of correctness.

7. **Baselines are illustrative**  
   Keyword/graph/TF-IDF-style baselines are intentionally crude comparators for pitch context, not peer-reviewed benchmarks.
